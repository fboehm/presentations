Quantifying the impact of reference panel sample size on polygenic score accuracy

Polygenic scores are widely used to quantify genetic liability for complex traits using genome-wide association study  summary statistics. A critical but under-examined component of many polygenic score construction methods is the linkage disequilibrium reference panel, whose sample size determines the fidelity with which correlation structure among genetic variants is estimated. Despite the widespread use of polygenic score methods with linkage disequilibrium reference panels, the impact of reference panel sample size on polygenic score accuracy remains poorly quantified.

In this work, we systematically investigate how linkage disequilibrium reference panel sample size influences polygenic score accuracy across a range of genetic architectures, trait types, ancestral groups, and modeling assumptions. Using both simulations and real genome-wide association study summary statistics, we construct polygenic scores under varying reference panel sizes while holding genome-wide association study sample size fixed. We evaluate predictive accuracy in independent target samples for continuous and binary traits, and examine interactions with key factors including trait polygenicity, SNP heritability, and prior assumptions on effect size distributions.

Our findings provide practical guidance for selecting reference panels and clarify trade-offs between computational cost and predictive performance and inform the design and interpretation of polygenic scoring analyses in large-scale genetic studies.
